Shooting products for a 3D scan

How to photograph or film a product so Bozzetto can turn it into a closed 3D shape for Blender, from a single photo up to a full photogrammetry shoot. The software design is in design.html.

Bozzetto 0.2Windows · Linux · macOSGPL-3.0-or-later

Which photos each engine needs

Bozzetto picks its scanning engine from the photos you give it. Shoot for the engine you want:

EnginePhotosHow to shoot
TripoSR (one photo)1A sharp three-quarter view, product filling most of the frame, plain contrasting background. The hidden side is guessed.
Turntable silhouette12–30One full turn in equal steps (24 × 15° is ideal), camera fixed and tilted down about 20°, plain contrasting backdrop. Only the outline matters, so glossy products are fine.
Apple Object Capture (Mac, coming)20–80The full multi-ring shoot below.
COLMAP + OpenMVS (coming)30–100The full multi-ring shoot below, with cross-polarised light for glossy products.

Whatever the engine, measure the product’s real height with calipers and enter it in Bozzetto, so the model has true size.

The full shoot

2.1How many photos

What matters is coverage and overlap, not the raw count. Every part of the surface should appear sharply in at least three photos, and each photo should share about 70% of its view with the next.

ProductPhotosExamples
One-photo shape (TripoSR)1A plausible closed shape; the hidden side is guessed
Turntable outline (silhouette engine)12–30One full turn in equal steps; round products come out within about 1–2%
Simple shape40–60Lipstick bullet, round jar, soap bar
Typical60–80Perfume bottle, tube with a cap, compact
Complex80–120Pump bottle, hinged palette, brush set
Including the base+20–24One extra ring with the product flipped

The plan in this guide (three rings of 24 plus 4 top shots) gives 76 photos, enough for nearly any cosmetic product. Add the flipped ring for about 100.

What 90% accuracy means here

For a matte or lightly textured product shot this way, photogrammetry engines (Apple Object Capture, COLMAP + OpenMVS) typically land within about 1–2% of real dimensions, comfortably past 90%. Accuracy drops when reflections move between photos, when photos are soft, or when parts of the product were never seen.

Using a video instead of photos

If you don’t want to take dozens of photos, film a short clip and choose Video. Bozzetto pulls evenly spaced frames from it (about 2 per second by default; you can change the number). Around each point in time it looks at 3 nearby frames and keeps the sharpest, so a little hand shake doesn’t ruin the set. The frames are kept in Bozzetto’s cache folder and scanned in order.

Copies of the same photo don’t help: every engine except TripoSR needs the same spot seen from different camera positions. A video gives you that for free, because every frame is a new position.

  • One slow, full circle of 15–30 seconds, about an arm’s length away. A second, higher circle improves the top.
  • Or fix the camera and turn the product slowly on a turntable.
  • Lock exposure and focus (on an iPhone, long-press the product to set AE/AF lock). Shoot 4K if you can.
  • Move slowly and smoothly; motion blur is the main thing that spoils video frames.
  • The same lighting advice applies: soft, even, and cross-polarised for glossy packaging.

2.3Studio setup

  • Place the product in the exact centre of the turntable so it doesn’t swing across the frame.
  • Leave a little space between the product and the background so masking separates them cleanly.
  • Wipe the product: dust and fingerprints show up as texture and confuse matching.
  • Remove loose parts that may shift (tilted caps, labels peeling). Anything that moves during the shoot will blur in the model.

2.4Lighting

Photogrammetry wants flat, even, unchanging light. That is different from beauty lighting: no rim lights, no dramatic shadows, no specular highlights. Shadows and highlights move as the turntable turns, and the software reads them as surface detail.

  • Two large diffused lights at about 45° either side of the camera, equal power.
  • Optional soft fill from above to lift the top of the product.
  • Turn off room lights and window light that might change during the shoot.
  • Same light for every photo, including the flipped base ring.

Cross-polarisation for glossy packaging

Glossy jars, lacquered caps and metallic compacts reflect the lights, and those reflections slide across the surface as the product turns. Cross-polarisation removes them.

  1. Cover each light with linear polarising film, all sheets in the same orientation.
  2. Fit a circular polariser to the lens.
  3. Look through the camera at a shiny part of the product and rotate the lens polariser until the reflections disappear (about 90° to the film).
  4. Expect to lose about 1.5–2 stops of light; lengthen the shutter speed, not the ISO.
Matte finish matters more than photo count

On glossy products, cross-polarised light is the single biggest step toward an accurate model. Doubling the number of photos does not fix reflections.

When exporting from RAW: no cropping, no lens-profile corrections, no perspective changes, and no heavy sharpening. Gentle exposure and white-balance fixes are fine if applied the same way to every photo.

2.6The shoot, step by step

  1. Prepare

    Clean the product, centre it on the turntable, set the lights and polarisers, and lock the camera settings. Take one test shot and check it at 100% for sharpness and reflections.

  2. Ring 1: low (24 photos)

    Camera slightly above table level, 10–15° looking down. Shoot, turn 15°, shoot, until you are back at the start. This ring captures the sides and the lower edge.

  3. Ring 2: middle (24 photos)

    Raise the tripod to 30–35° looking down, re-centre the product in the frame without touching focus or zoom, and repeat the full turn.

  4. Ring 3: high (24 photos)

    Raise again to 55–60°. Repeat the full turn. Shoulders, caps and the top edge come from this ring.

  5. Top shots (4–6 photos)

    From almost directly above, at 90° turns of the turntable. These fill in flat tops and lids.

  6. Base (optional, 20–24 photos)

    Lay the product on its side or upside down in the same light and shoot one more ring at about 30°. Photogrammetry engines can join the two orientations. Skip this if the base will sit flat in your sculpture anyway; Bozzetto already places the base at y = 0.

  7. Review

    Flick through every photo at 100%. Delete blurry, mis-exposed or obstructed frames rather than keeping them. A gap of one missing photo is better than a soft one.

  8. Export

    Export full-resolution JPEGs (or keep HEICs) into one folder named after the product, for example lipstick/. Keep the original order of the files.

Scanning sprays

Vanishing sprays sublimate after a few hours and leave no residue, but test on a hidden spot first. They are not suitable for products you are going to photograph for sale the same day.

2.8Processing in Bozzetto

  1. Open Bozzetto, choose Photos or Video, and pick the folder or file. Check the photo count and the “Turntable shoot” note.
  2. Read the Engine line: Bozzetto shows which engine it picked and why. Choose another from the list if you prefer.
  3. Tick I measured the real height and type the calipers reading in mm.
  4. Turntable shoots: set the Camera tilt (degrees looking down) and tick clockwise if the turntable turned that way seen from above.
  5. Choose Quick shape first. If it looks complete, try Accurate.
  6. Read the log: photos where the outline was unclear are listed by name; reshoot those.

Importing into Blender

  • Open in Blender in Bozzetto does it for you when Blender is installed.
  • By hand: File → Import → Wavefront (.obj), keep Up = Y and Forward = −Z, and set Scale = 0.001 because the file is in millimetres.

2.9Checking accuracy

Measure three things on the real product with calipers and on the model in Blender (enable the Measure tool, or read the object’s Dimensions in the N panel):

Example figures for illustration. Height is fixed by the scaling, so judge accuracy on the other measurements. Under 5% error on each is a good scan; under 10% meets the 90% target.

2.11Shoot-day checklist

  • Product cleaned and centred on the turntable
  • Plain matte background, gap behind the product
  • Two diffused lights at 45°, room lights off
  • Polarisers set for glossy surfaces
  • Tripod locked, timer or remote on
  • Manual mode, f/8–f/11, ISO 100–200
  • Manual focus set, zoom fixed, stabilisation off
  • White balance fixed
  • Test shot checked at 100%
  • Ring 1 · low · 24 shots
  • Ring 2 · middle · 24 shots
  • Ring 3 · high · 24 shots
  • Top · 4–6 shots
  • Base ring (optional) · 20–24 shots
  • Blurry frames deleted
  • Full-size JPEGs in one folder
  • Height measured with calipers
  • Preview run, then Full
